MedGEN-Bench summary

MedGEN-Bench tests whether a system can follow image-specific clinical instructions and produce an open-ended text answer, an edited medical image, or both. The benchmark covers CT, MRI, ultrasound, radiography, pathology, and clinical photography; every retained pair is reviewed for anatomical accuracy, pathological plausibility, and clinical coherence.

Benchmark Overview

Three output formats expose different failures in multimodal medical reasoning.

Visual Question Answering

image + query → text

Multiple choice · Blank filling · Question answering · Report generation

Image Editing

image + instruction → image

Resolution · Contrast · Noise reconstruction · Artifact removal · Annotation · Style transfer

Contextual Multimodal Generation

image + context → image + text

Disease prediction · Instruction editing · Organ removal/reconstruction · 3D↔2D conversion

Modalities: CT · MRI · Ultrasound · X-ray / Radiography · Pathology · Clinical Photography

Current public release. The Hugging Face manifest reports 6,623 examples, 13,021 image references, and 11,105 unique referenced images, with zero missing references. These release figures are kept separate from the paper statistics above. Inspect the manifest.

Evaluation and Findings

Local quality scores are paired with holistic judgments of clinical cross-modal alignment.

Image level

SSIM · PSNR · LPIPS

Text level

PubMedBERT-based BERTScore

Holistic level

Coherence · Alignment · Accuracy · Relevance · Consistency
  • Evaluation coverage: 10 compositional frameworks, 2 image-editing models, 3 unified models, and 5 VLMs.
  • Compositional frameworks perform better overall on cross-modal consistency.
  • Strong local image metrics can mask weak multimodal reasoning.
  • Across 1,000 sampled pairs, contextual augmentation increases mean instruction–image similarity from 0.273 to 0.372 (+36.3%), with an average pass rate of 86.9%.

The 36.3% result measures semantic alignment, not diagnostic accuracy.
